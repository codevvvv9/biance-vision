## The research question

In this invented range, price has moved above the upper boundary. Is this a lasting change of context or another failed breakout? The useful distinction is between the initial attempt and subsequent acceptance. The case stops at conditional reasoning; no successful outcome is attached to it.

Assume a range with an identified upper boundary and a closed candle above it. The diagram is synthetic and contains no live prices. An open candle briefly above the boundary would be a weaker observation than the stated close; neither observation determines the next candle.

## Supporting evidence and counterevidence

The close beyond the boundary supports investigating a breakout interpretation. A later retest could help distinguish acceptance from rejection. The opposing explanation is a failed attempt: prior reversals and limited follow-through may still matter. A retest that has not happened cannot be cited as evidence already available.

## What weakens the interpretation

A return into the range weakens the breakout thesis, particularly if subsequent closed observations fail to regain the boundary. An isolated tick should be described as such. Define the relevant horizon before judging whether acceptance is sustained. Waiting for evidence does not mean assuming every breakout will fail.
