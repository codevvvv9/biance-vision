## Begin with the information boundary

A result describes a market snapshot, not an indefinitely valid view. Check the pair, market, horizon and snapshot time. A live price is a different observation from the closed candles used to establish a structure.

If the market has materially changed, revisit the research instead of treating an earlier conclusion as current.

## A conclusion is conditional

Read what must hold for the interpretation to remain useful. A directional view, a risk review and a “wait” conclusion answer different questions; none should be reduced to a certainty about the next candle.

## Read both sides of the evidence

Supporting evidence explains why an interpretation is plausible. Opposing evidence explains why it may fail. Follow the evidence to the relevant chart area or source; the number of citations alone does not establish their strength.

## Know when to stop using the result

Invalidation conditions identify changes that require a new assessment. They are part of the original reasoning, not an explanation added after an outcome. Missing or stale data is a separate limitation and should be stated explicitly.
