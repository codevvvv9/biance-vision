## Freeze the information boundary

A research result belongs to a particular market, pair, horizon and snapshot time. The terminal distinguishes closed-candle observations from a separately timed live quote. A later price cannot be used to justify an observation supposedly available earlier. Check timestamps and missing inputs before reading a directional interpretation.

## Read structure in context

A trend, range, pullback or breakout attempt is a description of context. It is not a complete entry decision. Distinguish the candle being described from the later candle that confirms a structure. More overlapping candles or contradictory horizons may require a weaker conclusion rather than a more confident narrative.

## Keep the strongest opposing explanation

For each view, ask which observation supports it, which observation challenges it and where that observation can be checked. A citation only helps if it supports the associated claim. Supporting and opposing evidence are not votes; one important contradiction can outweigh several weak observations.

## Validate inputs and disclose gaps

The research workflow checks structured inputs and the consistency of the result. It cannot manufacture an absent quote, a reliable probability or missing market data. A completed validation step does not guarantee the market interpretation is correct. When required information is missing, state the gap and narrow the conclusion.

## State invalidation before the next move

A useful conclusion says what would weaken it and when another snapshot is needed. Distinguish a structural condition from a transient tick, using the stated horizon and confirmation rule. Waiting is appropriate when competing explanations remain unresolved. These are research conditions, not automatic orders or a promise of profit.
